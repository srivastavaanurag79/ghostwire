# GhostWire, explained like you've never used a computer

**You don't need to know anything about technology to read this page.** Imagine I'm explaining it to a friend over coffee.

---

## What is GhostWire?

GhostWire is a **private group chat** for phones and computers.

But it's a very unusual kind of group chat, because of three simple rules:

1. **There is no company in the middle.** No Facebook, no WhatsApp, no Google. Nobody's server. Nothing to sign up for.
2. **Nothing is saved.** When the chat is over, it's gone. There's no history to scroll back to, and nothing left behind for anyone to find.
3. **It can work with no internet at all.** If the internet is down, or switched off, it can still work between phones that are near each other.

Think of it like passing notes around a circle of friends instead of mailing letters through the post office. The notes never go through anyone else's hands, and when everyone leaves, the notes are destroyed.

---

## Why would anyone want this?

Normal chat apps are like sending letters through a post office. The post office (the company) is very convenient, but it:
- needs to be open (the internet has to work),
- keeps a copy of your letters (your messages are stored),
- can tell who wrote to whom, and when.

For most everyday chats that's fine. But sometimes you want something more like **whispering in a circle**:
- a protest or a march where the internet might be shut off,
- a group planning something private,
- a place with no phone signal,
- or you simply don't want a big company holding your conversations.

GhostWire is built for those moments.

---

## How do you use it? (It's this easy)

Think of it like setting up a **walkie-talkie channel**.

1. **One person opens GhostWire** on their phone or computer and taps **"Create a session."** They become the host (the group leader).
2. **The app shows a code** — either a **square barcode** (called a QR code) that another phone can point its camera at, or a **short number**, like `483920`.
3. **Everyone else opens GhostWire**, taps **"Join,"** and either **scans the square code** or **types the number**.
4. **They're in.** They can type messages to the group. That's it.
5. **When you're done, you close the session.** Everything disappears. There's no "delete history" button, because there's no history in the first place.

There's also a big red **"Panic Wipe"** button. If you tap it, GhostWire instantly erases everything from your device's memory and starts fresh. It's like burning the notes.

---

## Who can talk, and who can only read?

Sometimes you want some people to talk and others to just watch — like a classroom, or a live event where only the organisers should speak. So GhostWire has simple roles:

- **Host (Admin)** — the person who created the session. They can let people in, and they can remove troublemakers.
- **Moderator** — a trusted helper the host appoints. They can let people in too.
- **Speaker** — can send messages.
- **Listener** — can only read. They can't type. (Even if they tried to sneak a message in, everyone else's app would ignore it.)

You don't have to set anything up. When someone joins, the host just picks which role they get.

---

## "But does it need the internet?"

Here's the neat part: **it doesn't have to.**

There are three ways people can connect:

1. **Same Wi-Fi or hotspot.** If phones are on the same Wi-Fi (or one phone shares its hotspot), they can talk directly to each other, even if the internet is completely down.
2. **A tiny helper on your own device.** If you want the "type a number" style, one device runs a small helper program that passes messages between everyone on the network. It never reads the messages — it's just a mail sorter.
3. **Bluetooth (on the phone app).** This is the big one for emergencies: phones can talk to each other **directly over Bluetooth**, with no internet, no Wi-Fi, and no router. Just phones near each other.

That last one is why GhostWire exists. During an internet shutdown, when everyone has a phone but nothing else works, GhostWire is meant to keep a group connected.

---

## Is it actually private?

Yes — and here's what that means in plain words:

- **Messages are scrambled** before they leave your device and only unscrambled on the other person's device. If someone in the middle tried to look, they'd see nonsense.
- **Even the names are hidden** inside the scrambled part. Someone watching the network traffic can't easily tell *who* sent *what*.
- **Nothing is stored** — not on a server (there isn't one), and not on your phone after the session ends.
- **No tracking, no ads, no analytics.** GhostWire doesn't phone home. It doesn't even tell us you exist, because it never contacts us.

The keys to unlock the messages are created fresh for each session and thrown away when you're done.

---

## Be honest with yourself: what it *can't* do

GhostWire is powerful, but it's not magic. A responsible friend would tell you:

- If someone you invited is mean or untrustworthy, they can still copy your messages or take screenshots. **Only invite people you trust.**
- If your phone is already infected with spyware *while* the chat is open, that spyware could see what's on your screen. No app can fully prevent that.
- The host can see who joined. That's part of their job.
- It's not a cloak of invisibility. Someone standing next to you can still see your screen.

Use it like you'd use any important conversation: with care and with people you trust.

## Where do I get it?

- **In a web browser:** go to the live link (in the [main README](./README.md)). You can "install" it to your home screen like a normal app, and it even works offline after the first time.
- **On an Android phone:** download the app file (APK) and tap it to install. (The README has the link.)
- **From source:** for the technically curious, everything is free and open on [GitHub](https://github.com/srivastavaanurag79/ghostwire).

## How can I help?

GhostWire is a free, open project (MIT licensed). If you're a developer, a designer, a translator, or just someone who cares about private communication that survives shutdowns, you're welcome to help. The [main README](./README.md) and [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](./CONTRIBUTING.md) explain how.

If you only take one thing away, let it be this:

> **GhostWire is a group chat that lives only in the moment — no company, no records, and it keeps working even when the internet doesn't.**

That's the whole idea. Everything else is just how we get there.
